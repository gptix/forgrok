* Top recommendations
The original, section =Top 3 Recommendations=, ranks three options. Section =Summary= states the same order.

** 1. AI automation services (Make.com / n8n + AI)
From the original, heading =1. AI Automation Services (Make.com / n8n + AI)=:

- Offer productized, fixed-price automation packages to small businesses: connect apps, automate repetitive workflows, and add AI to processes such as email filing, CRM updates, invoice processing, lead intake, and social scheduling, then move to a monthly retainer after setup.
- Startup path described: Make.com free plan, self-hosted n8n, an optional paid LLM tier, and a free or low-cost landing page. The brief says no coding is required and that basics can be learned in a few days from free tutorials.
- First-customer methods in the original (warm outreach, a free pilot, and freelance-platform gigs) are under =How to Find First Customers=. Local group and meetup suggestions stay in the original.
- Main risks named there: slow first client, underpricing, scope creep, and being too general. Mitigations offered: start outreach immediately, use fixed-price packages, and pick one vertical.
- A four-week launch plan is under =30-Day Launch Plan= in that same recommendation.

** 2. Freelance AI copywriting / content services
From the original, heading =2. Freelance AI Copywriting / Content Services=:

- Write blog posts, email sequences, social content, and website copy for small businesses, using AI tools for drafting and a human for strategy, editing, brand voice, and oversight, packaged as retainers.
- Startup path described: optional paid LLM tier, free docs and portfolio, optional domain. The brief stresses editing skill and a few practice samples before pitching.
- First-customer methods (emailing businesses with weak existing content, freelance platforms, community groups, and a local association) are in that section; named local pages stay in the original.
- Main risks named there: commodity pricing, weak differentiation, and clients who do not value writing as such. Mitigations offered: pick an industry niche, sell outcomes rather than "writing," and lead with strategy and editorial quality.
- A four-week launch plan is under =30-Day Launch Plan= in that recommendation.

** 3. Online flipping (eBay / Facebook Marketplace)
From the original, heading =3. Online Flipping (eBay / Facebook Marketplace)=:

- Buy and resell goods on eBay and Facebook Marketplace, starting with items already on hand, then reinvesting into small sourced inventory.
- The brief says this can reach a first sale fastest of the three, but it involves sourcing, shipping, and sales-tax complexity that the service businesses largely avoid.
- Main risks named there: overpaying for inventory, slow shipping and bad feedback, unsold items, storage clutter, and sales-tax bookkeeping. Mitigations offered: check sold comps before buying, ship promptly with tracking, list on more than one platform, cap inventory, and rely on marketplace facilitator tax collection where it applies.
- A four-week launch plan is under =30-Day Launch Plan= in that recommendation.

* Other candidates
The original, section =All Candidates Compared=, also scores virtual assistant work, prompt-engineering consulting, print on demand, SEO consulting, an affiliate niche site, local window cleaning, and a sponsor-based newsletter.

Qualitative reasons given in that section, without repeating the brief's numeric targets:

- Virtual assistant work is treated as a viable runner-up that typically pays less at first and competes with offshore labor. See also the original, section =Summary=.
- Prompt-engineering consulting is scored near flipping but is not in the top three.
- Print on demand and affiliate or newsletter projects are scored low because of slow ramp and tight or uncertain early revenue.
- SEO consulting is scored lower because of a long ramp, despite decent later income potential.
- Window cleaning is scored lower because of equipment cost, local travel, and weaker fit with an AI or automation interest.

* Georgia legal and tax notes
The original has a full section, =Georgia / Cobb Legal and Tax Notes=, plus a shorter licensing subsection under each recommendation. Those pages include county-level license, zoning, and outreach detail that this summary does not repeat.

State-level points the brief states, all in that legal section unless noted:

- A sole proprietorship needs no Georgia state registration; income is reported on the personal return (GA Form 500 / Schedule C). An LLC can be formed later; the brief cites a $110 Secretary of State filing fee.
- A trade name (DBA) is needed only if operating under a name other than the legal name. Where to file, and the fee, are in the original and marked UNVERIFIED as to cost.
- The brief states that Georgia does not charge sales tax on professional services such as consulting, copywriting, and automation work, only on tangible goods.
- If reselling goods, the brief says Georgia sales-and-use tax registration is required for in-state sales, and that eBay may collect marketplace facilitator tax on its platform (confirm; marked UNVERIFIED). The local combined rate quoted in the original stays there.
- The brief cites a 5.39% Georgia flat income-tax rate for 2026, and mentions quarterly estimates if tax owed is expected to exceed a stated threshold (see the original).
- General liability and professional liability are described as optional at the start, especially for desk-based services, with published monthly ranges in the original, section =Insurance=.
