* Definition

High-Protein Content (HPC) is dense, high-signal material that carries lasting intellectual or practical value. It is deliberately contrasted with high-volume, low-density internet content.

This is the first of the three explicit mission pillars of the Zero-Human Organization Intent.

* Core distinction

| High-Protein                                      | Low-Protein                                      |
|---------------------------------------------------|--------------------------------------------------|
| Primary sources, careful empirical work           | Aggregated commentary, secondary summaries       |
| Older technical literature & engineering handbooks| Ephemeral social media and news cycles           |
| Court opinions, statutes, original research       | SEO-driven listicles and recycled takes          |
| Pre-internet or carefully curated long-form work  | High-volume, low-signal web content              |

The distinction originates in Brian Roemmele’s writing and is treated as an operational filter for this knowledge base and for any future autonomous collection agents.

* Why it matters

- Maximizes the long-term value of the knowledge graph.
- Reduces noise that would otherwise dilute Withnail’s reasoning and the operator’s attention.
- Provides a clear, auditable inclusion criterion for both human and agent-driven ingest.
- Aligns the entire system (org-roam, selective exposure, Roemmele ingest pipeline) around signal rather than volume.

* Current practice (as of 2026-08)

- Roemmele essay ingest pipeline is the primary working example of systematic HPC collection.
- Mechanical-first flow: HTML → cleaned markdown + images → full org-roam node written by Grok → human review → push.
- Essays 001–007 fully processed and marked pushed.
- Broader HPC sources live under =04-sources/= (roemmele/, wilhelm/, hpc/).

* Relationship to the other two pillars

- **Save-Your-Wisdom**: HPC principles also guide what personal and professional knowledge is worth capturing and structuring.
- **Public Arrestee Data**: Primary public records (court dockets, arrest records already released under open-records law) are treated as high-protein source material when properly provenance-tracked.
