* Summary
A personal tribute: he says his dear friend John Nosta has died following an
aortic aneurysm, and offers prayers for Amy and children Katie, Annabel, Jack,
and the wider family. The long post sketches a path he says he knew from their
talks and the public record: university work at sixteen (advanced Harvard summer
program; father an electrical engineer in Perth Amboy who wanted a doctor);
service as president of the Perth Amboy First Aid Squad and paramedic training
among Spanish-speaking neighbors; a self-designed biophysics concentration at
Boston University; a research-associate year at Harvard Medical School on cell
volume, infarction, arrhythmias and sudden cardiac death under mentors including
Thomas Smith; undergraduate-era papers in American Journal of Physiology,
Circulation, and the American Journal of Cardiology around 1981; declining an
MD/PhD path to finish the BU degree and move into writing and translating science.
He says Nosta wrote more than a thousand pieces on technology, medicine, and the
mind, and that The Borrowed Mind carried the same insistence that human thought
stays the author's even when tools grow fluent. He calls him a national-treasure
genius and closes with rest in peace.

Follow-ups link one of Nosta's lectures on The Borrowed Mind and a TEDx talk
("brilliant"); neither media was opened here. A reply to @joelcomm restates that
he was a brilliant mind and a good friend.
