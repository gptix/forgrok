* Summary
His X article "Vibration, Nitric Oxide, and Brain Fluid Clearance: What the New
Oulu Study Actually Shows" opens with a 1965 Magic Fingers motel vignette, then
immediately warns: research coverage only, not medical advice; sublingual
nitroglycerin can sharply lower blood pressure and must not be used outside
clinical supervision; neither vibrating mattresses, nitric oxide donors, nor
light-and-sound devices are shown here to treat, prevent, or reverse Alzheimer's.

He summarizes a 3 Nov 2026 open-access Nature Communications paper (Tuunanen et
al., senior author Vesa Kiviniemi): randomized crossover in 29 healthy adults
(mean age ~44). Arms included combined sublingual glyceryl trinitrate (0.5 mg)
plus Piezo1-targeted whole-body vibration (~37–40 min, Neurosonic mattress,
~22–50 Hz), vibration alone, and a nitric-oxide-only subset. After hemodilution
correction, combined-session blood rises included Aβ40, Aβ42 (~35% mean rise as
press coverage framed it), and GFAP; larger proteins (NfL, p-tau181) did not
change significantly. BP fell ~15%; the drop size correlated with protein rises;
older volunteers showed stronger signals. T2 dark-fluid MRI showed whole-brain
signal intensity down ~4.6%, extracerebral CSF volume up ~6.5 ml, and posterior
venous-sinus signal changes correlating with circulating Aβ—read by the authors
as consistent with transient redistribution of soluble brain proteins toward
venous blood, not as disease modification. Combination affected a wider imaging
volume than either stimulus alone; a sham rest cohort did not match the pattern.
Mild headaches/nausea occurred; one discontinuation after vomiting; no serious
adverse events under hospital conditions. Limits he stresses: no placebo pill;
healthy volunteers only; no cognition, plaque, or prevention endpoints; DIY
explicitly unsafe (Kiviniemi warning on hypotension).

Two short promo posts quote the article—one with the Magic Fingers joke, one
headlined "Alzheimer's breakthrough"—which is harsher marketing than the article's
own careful bottom line.

* Key claims
- Acute biomarker/MRI signals in healthy adults under supervision (as the paper
  and his article frame them); not proof of Alzheimer's treatment or prevention.
- Combined NO + vibration > either alone on spatial MRI extent (per his summary).
- Primary paper, New Scientist, Oulu/Newswise, and Neurosonic pages cited in the
  article were not independently opened here.

* Practical takeaways
- None clinical. Do not experiment with nitroglycerin or vibration protocols.
- Useful only as a carefully hedged research note next to his prior LED/
  entrainment writing; keep the medical disclaimer if ever referenced.
