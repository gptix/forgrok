* Summary
He retells Thomas Edison's early-1900s plan for affordable housing: reusable
cast-iron molds to pour complete concrete homes (including bathtubs) in about six
hours via the Edison Portland Cement Company, pitched against crowded, fire-prone
tenements. As he frames it, the mold for one design used more than 2,300
interlocking nickel-plated iron pieces; contractors needed roughly $175,000 in
equipment (1907 dollars); setup/teardown needed large crews; the pour demanded
concrete that flowed from the roof without voids; and the public branded the
houses as slum/uniform boxes before many were built. Only a few dozen went up
(mainly Union, NJ and Gary, IN), and Edison took a multimillion-dollar loss.
