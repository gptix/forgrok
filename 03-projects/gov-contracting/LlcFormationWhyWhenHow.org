* Purpose of this node

Synthesizes:
- Official IRS and SBA guidance

- Mark J. Kohler’s practical framework (from the 2025 video “I’ve Set Up Over 50,000 LLCs. Here’s What NOT To Do!” and related writings)
- Consensus from reputable practitioner sources

* Why form an LLC

** Primary reasons (liability + separation)
- Creates a separate legal entity so that, in most cases, business debts and lawsuits do not reach personal assets (home, personal bank accounts, etc.).
- Provides clearer separation between personal and business finances, which supports clean bookkeeping, banking, and future tax elections.
- Increases credibility with clients, partners, banks, and government contracting officers (especially useful for SAM.gov / Team Georgia Marketplace registration).

** Tax reality (important)
- An LLC by itself does **not** automatically save income tax.
- Default federal tax treatment:
  - Single-member LLC → disregarded entity (reported on owner’s Schedule C / personal return).
  - Multi-member LLC → partnership (Form 1065) unless it elects otherwise.
- The LLC *can* later elect to be taxed as an S corporation (Form 2553) once net profits justify the extra formalities and reasonable-salary requirement. Common rule-of-thumb threshold cited by practitioners: roughly $40k–$60k+ net profit.
- Self-employment tax still applies to pass-through income until an S-Corp election is properly made and a reasonable salary is paid.

** Secondary benefits
- Easier path to a dedicated business bank account and EIN.
- Cleaner branding and contracts (sign as the LLC, not personally).
- Foundation for later complexity (multiple members, investment holdings, trust ownership).

* When to form an LLC

** Strong triggers
- You are about to sign contracts, leases, or service agreements in a business capacity.
- You are taking on any meaningful liability exposure (clients, employees, physical products/services, driving for business, etc.).
- You are generating (or about to generate) revenue and want clean separation.
- You plan to pursue government contracting, vendor registration, or professional services where an entity is expected or advantageous.
- You have (or expect) personal assets worth protecting.

** Weaker / premature triggers
- Pure idea-testing or hobby-level activity with negligible revenue and almost no liability.
- Forming “just because” or because an online service marketed Wyoming/Nevada privacy benefits.

** Timing guidance (Kohler + consensus)
- Form the operational LLC in the home state as soon as the activity is real enough to need liability protection or a bank account/EIN.
- Do **not** form too early if the only result is extra annual fees and paperwork with no corresponding activity.
- Plan for a possible S-Corp election later in the same tax year or the following year once profits are clear; the LLC can be the vehicle that later elects S status.

* How to form an LLC (practical sequence)

** 1. Decide purpose and type
- **Operational LLC** — active business / services / side hustle / contracting vehicle. Form in the state where you live and primarily operate (Georgia).
- **Investment / holding LLC** — owns assets (real estate, equipment, etc.). Form where the asset is located or where protection is needed.
- Avoid the common marketing trap of forming the *operational* company in Wyoming, Nevada, or Delaware “for privacy or asset protection.” For most small operators this creates extra cost, foreign qualification requirements, and later cleanup. (Wyoming-style entities have niche uses in advanced multi-layer planning for higher-net-worth situations.)

** 2. Georgia-specific formation (home-state operational entity)
- File Articles of Organization with the Georgia Secretary of State (online preferred).
- Required elements typically include: LLC name (must contain LLC or equivalent), principal office address, registered agent (Georgia street address), organizer(s).
- Filing fee ≈ $100 + service charge (confirm current amount on sos.ga.gov). Expedited options available.
- Annual registration required (currently $60, due Jan 1–Apr 1).
- Official sources: Georgia Secretary of State Corporations Division forms and online portal.

** 3. Core supporting documents and steps
- **Operating Agreement** — strongly recommended even when not legally required by the state. Defines ownership, management, distributions, decision-making, and what happens on death/exit. Critical for single-member LLCs as well (proves separateness).
- **Registered Agent** — required; can be yourself (with a physical Georgia address) or a commercial service (better for privacy).
- **EIN** — apply free at IRS.gov (Form SS-4). Needed for banking, employees, and most multi-member or electing entities. Many single-member disregarded LLCs still obtain one for practical reasons.
- **Manager-managed vs Member-managed** — Manager-managed is generally preferred for privacy (owners are not listed as publicly on formation documents in the same way).
- Do **not** list spouse or children as members unless you intentionally want partnership tax treatment and are prepared for Form 1065 complexity.
- Open a dedicated business bank account in the LLC’s name.
- Keep basic records / minutes to maintain the liability shield (avoid “alter ego” piercing arguments).

** 4. Longer-term structure (Kohler “Trifecta” framework)
- Foundation: Revocable Living Trust (owns the LLCs over time; handles legacy and privacy).
- Left side: Operational LLC (Georgia, home-state, path to S-Corp).
- Right side: Investment LLC(s) as assets accumulate.
- The trust does not have to exist on day one, but the design should point toward it.

* Sources (top-notch / primary)

** Official
- IRS — Limited Liability Company (LLC) overview, single-member LLC rules, entity classification (Form 8832), Publication 3402 (Taxation of LLCs), Publication 583 (Starting a Business and Keeping Records).
- U.S. Small Business Administration — Choose a business structure, Register your business, operating agreement guidance.
- Georgia Secretary of State — Articles of Organization (CD 030), online filing portal, annual registration requirements, registered agent rules.

** Practitioner (high-volume experience)
- Mark J. Kohler, CPA & Attorney — “I’ve Set Up Over 50,000 LLCs. Here’s What NOT To Do!” (YouTube, 2025) and related Trifecta writings on markjkohler.com / KKOS Lawyers. Emphasizes home-state operational LLCs, avoidance of unnecessary Wyoming entities for ordinary businesses, proper ownership, and operating agreements.

** Supporting consensus sources
- Investopedia, Forbes Advisor, and major CPA firm guidance that align with the IRS/SBA position: form in the state of operations, prioritize liability protection, treat the operating agreement as essential, and time S-Corp election to actual profits.

* Decision checklist for current projects

- [ ] Confirm primary activity is operational (services, contracting, software/app) rather than pure asset holding.
- [ ] Form Georgia domestic LLC for the main operating entity.
- [ ] Use manager-managed structure and keep ownership simple (single member or trust).
- [ ] Draft / obtain a real operating agreement.
- [ ] Obtain EIN and open business bank account.
- [ ] Plan (but do not force) future S-Corp election once net profits support it.
- [ ] Defer or carefully scope any Wyoming / multi-state entities until clear advanced need exists.
- [ ] Align eventual ownership with a revocable living trust.
