#+DESCRIPTION: Project to give Mom (84, Atlanta) reliable home phone service that works indoors and during power outages.

* Goal
Give Mom a reliable home phone that works indoors despite poor cell reception, and keeps working during power outages.

* Situation (as of 2026-10-27)
- Mom: 84, Atlanta, retired public school teacher; USAA and AAA member.
- Cell reception inside the house is terrible.
- Has internet and TV, believed to be Comcast/Xfinity.
- Uses an iPhone; its bill is believed bundled with the TV/internet bill (likely Xfinity Mobile — confirm).

* Options investigated
** 1. iPhone Wi-Fi Calling (first step, likely free)
- Settings > Phone > Wi-Fi Calling. Calls route over home internet; full indoor coverage.
- Xfinity Mobile supports it. Depends on internet and Wi-Fi being up.
** 2. VoIP home line: Ooma Telo
- Base plugs into the router; standard corded or multi-handset cordless phones plug into it.
- Roughly $6–9/month in taxes and fees on the free tier.
- Multi-handset DECT cordless set covers several rooms without extra wiring.
** 3. Powerline adapters (e.g., Asoka PlugLink) — optional
- Carry network over house wiring; useful only if the phone base must sit far from the router.
- Not needed for extensions if a multi-handset cordless set is used.
** 4. Cell signal booster (weBoost, Cel-Fi)
- About $300–600 whole-home; needs usable signal outside the house.
- Fallback if Wi-Fi Calling is unsatisfactory.
** Age/affiliation discounts
- Checked age, retired-teacher, USAA, and AAA angles; no decisive landline discount found. Revisit if pursuing a traditional carrier line.

* Power-outage resilience
- Put modem, router, and phone base on a small UPS (battery backup).
- Keep one corded phone, or a cordless set whose base has battery backup.

* Recommended plan
1. Turn on Wi-Fi Calling on her iPhone; confirm carrier on the bill.
2. Add Ooma Telo with a multi-handset cordless set (plus one corded phone).
3. Put modem, router, and Ooma on a UPS.
4. Consider a booster only if the above falls short.
