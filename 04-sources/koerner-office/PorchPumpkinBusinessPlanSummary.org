* What the business is

Design, deliver, and install a *fixed menu* of fall pumpkin displays on residential porches. Full payment up front (Venmo in year one). Season is about two months of hard physical work, then pickup.

Heather's path:

- Inspired by Dallas Arboretum Pumpkin Village; decorated her own porch; neighbors asked.
- Season 1 goal: 30 orders for Christmas money. Hit 30 in *four days*. Finished at *250 homes, $100k revenue, ~50% margin*, no website.
- Season 6: ~*1,300 orders/year*, peak-season profit *>$100k per month*, margin 50–55%, ~20 farm-direct 18-wheeler loads.

Honest constraint in the plan: margins are earned in a deadline sprint, not passive income.

* Why the model works (plan's claims)

- Upfront pay → almost no collections risk.
- Short season + high visual shareability → word of mouth and organic social do most of the selling.
- Fixed packages beat custom quoting (restaurant-menu analogy).
- More local competitors *increase* demand (neighborhoods learn the category exists). Atlanta already has Praise Pumpkins and Spillers Pots; that is category education, not a reason to skip sourcing.

* Package menu (Heather, DFW — not Atlanta retail)

| Package | Price | Notes |
| Package One | $1,350 | Largest; design included |
| Package Two | $800 | Design included |
| Oh My Gourd | $750 | Neutral / non-orange colorway; design included |
| Package Three | $525 | Design +$75 (~95% take it); pickup +$100 (~90%) → typical ticket ~$700 |
| Package Four | $325 | Smallest; same add-ons |
| Custom | $3,500+ | Floor set high so custom does not eat the menu |

Sales move: offer *two* options after a short read of the house / event, not the whole menu.

Atlanta comps already in this session: Praise Pumpkins packages ~$375–$1,350 serving Marietta / Vinings / East Cobb; Spillers Pots porch packages from ~$500–$750+. Same shape, different brand.

* Four-tier sourcing (the useful part for Atlanta)

| Tier | Source | Trade-off |
| 1 | Grocery displays | Easy, 3–4 varieties, not scalable, worst margin |
| 2 | Nursery / garden center | Better fruit, still marked up |
| 3 | Broker / produce distributor | Convenient; pay the middleman. Heather *started here*. |
| 4 | Direct farm | Best price and mix; real minimums. Heather now: 20 truckloads. |

Named farm in the plan: *Pumpkin Pyle* (West Texas) — quality, ordering system, communication. That is a DFW freight relationship, not a default for Cobb.

Atlanta mapping from this session (not in the Koerner text):

- Tier 3 local: Atlanta State Farmers Market, Forest Park (Hammond's, Pumpkin Master historically; Harvest Moon can source). Walk the sheds.
- Tier 4 on the Marietta→Clayton / GA-365 corridor: *Jaemor Farms* (Alto) — Georgia Grown lists pumpkins wholesale *and* retail; ~200 acres historically; call weekday, do not treat the maze gate as wholesale.
- Tier 4 south GA with 2026 dates: Herrington Farms, Vienna — mixed/custom color bins, delivery quotes, ready week of 7 Sep 2026.
- Near Athens, not on the Clayton road: Lord of the Harvest / Hollisaja, Good Hope — bins and trailers; order window historically through ~10 Sep.

Plan guidance for a first season: start at nursery or broker volume. Do not buy a farm-minimum trailer until bookings justify the buffer.

* Unit economics (one real route)

Six Package Three stops on one flatbed:

- ~$700 typical ticket × 6 ≈ *$3,600* route revenue
- ~2.5–3 hours
- ~30 pumpkins on the trailer
- Example $600 job (no hay, stairs): ~$300 profit (~50%)
- Heather designs in ~10 minutes/house — design speed is a capacity lever

Think in *routes*, not only orders, once volume exists.

* Team and costs

- Hire drivers and designers from *customers* who already like the work.
- Drivers bring their own truck/trailer (including dump trailer for pickup). Fuel is on them.
- Designers: piece rate.
- Scale-only costs: umbrella liability, forklift rental, warehouse, owned trailers, optional paid social boosts.
- First season can be a personal vehicle or one borrowed trailer. Outdoor covered storage is enough at small volume.

Legal note in the plan: carry general liability. Confirm GA requirements separately.

* Marketing and booking

Year one: word of mouth + organic photos + Venmo. No site.
Follow-up discipline is treated as the real conversion engine (interest ≠ paid order).
Over-order inventory vs booked demand; hold a waitlist to soak surplus.
Pickup at season end is a paid add-on, not a favor.

* 30–60–90 (compressed to the pumpkin calendar)

- *Early summer:* freeze 3–5 packages; line up nursery/broker; do not launch custom-only.
- *Late summer → first bookings:* take money up front; watch color trends (white / heirloom vs all-orange); stay on the menu.
- *Delivery season:* route by geography; hire from the book; sell pickup; log actual demand so next year's tier (broker vs farm) is a number, not a hope.

* Financial snapshots from the plan

| Scenario | Revenue | Profit |
| Conservative new operator, S1 | $30–40k | $15–20k |
| Heather actual S1 | $100k | ~$50k |
| Heather S6 peak month | not given as annual | $100k+/month in season |

Do not treat DFW $100k S1 as an Atlanta promise. Use $30–40k as the plan's own "focused first season" target.

* Risks the plan names

Demand explosion (30 → 250) overwhelming a solo founder; weather; fruit quality; under-pricing custom work; skipping follow-up.

* Atlanta / this session — what to do with it

This document is a *playbook*, not a supplier list. For decoration this autumn:

1. Decide whether you are *buying fruit* (wholesale bins) or *selling installs* (Heather's business).
2. If buying fruit: Jaemor weekday call + Forest Park walk + Herrington quote. That is tiers 3–4 locally.
3. If selling installs: copy the menu/add-on structure, not the DFW prices, and treat Praise Pumpkins / Spillers as proof of local demand.
4. Do not start at 20 truckloads.
