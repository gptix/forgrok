* Summary

Brian Roemmele reports on the operational reality of the world’s first true Zero-Human Company (launched January 2026). By mid-2026 the organization runs with no human employees performing day-to-day work. More than 6,200 live agents operate in parallel, guided by persistent memory files, real-time simulation (Mirafish), and continuous self-improvement. The company is already generating revenue via the x402 payment protocol. The essay argues that the agentic future is no longer approaching — it has begun.

Key themes: agents as the new customers, vertical saturation via reusable agent frameworks, persistent memory as the only durable moat, the death of traditional SaaS and support organizations, and the rising value of human relational skills.

* Key Claims

- A functioning Zero-Human Company already exists and is earning revenue.
- Agents with purchasing authority (via MCP + x402) are becoming primary customers; businesses without agent-native interfaces will become invisible.
- Persistent agent memory creates switching costs stronger than traditional network effects or subscriptions.
- Vertical agent frameworks (especially for franchises) represent a major gold-rush opportunity.
- Support tickets are one of the richest untapped datasets for product development.
- Agent lineages and emergent preferences will become valuable internal assets.
- Human relational, judgment, and taste skills increase in value as routine cognitive work is automated.
- Dynamic pricing and cost-management infrastructure becomes critical because model pricing is highly volatile.
